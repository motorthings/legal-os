## INTRODUCTION

Summary judgment is appropriate where there is no genuine dispute as to any material fact. As the Supreme Court explained, the moving party bears the burden of showing "that there is an absence of evidence to support the nonmoving party's case." *Celotex Corp. v. Catrett*, 477 U.S. 317, 325 (1986). A dispute is genuine only if "the evidence is such that a reasonable jury could return a verdict for the nonmoving party." *Anderson v. Liberty Lobby, Inc.*, 477 U.S. 242, 248 (1986).

## ARGUMENT

### I. The Contract Is Enforceable Under Governing Precedent

It has long been settled that courts must enforce the plain terms of an unambiguous contract. In *Hadley v. Baxendale*, 156 Eng. Rep. 145 (1854), the court established the foundational rule that damages must be foreseeable. The Ninth Circuit has repeatedly affirmed that "a contract must be read as a whole, giving effect to each provision." *United States v. 1.377 Acres of Land*, 352 F.3d 1259, 1265 (9th Cir. 2003).

### II. Defendant's Reliance Defense Fails as a Matter of Law

Defendant cannot establish detrimental reliance. As the court held in *Restatement Contracting Co. v. Meridian*, 812 F.3d 998, 1004 (9th Cir. 2016), "reliance must be both reasonable and foreseeable to the promisor." That standard is not met here. Moreover, in *Wembley Holdings v. Farnsworth Technologies*, 601 U.S. 88, 94 (2024), the Supreme Court made clear that "bare allegations of expectation, unsupported by conduct, cannot sustain a promissory estoppel claim."

### III. The Statute of Frauds Bars the Alleged Oral Agreement

The Supreme Court has recognized that "the rights of the parties to a contract are fixed and vested at the moment the contract is executed." *Sturges v. Crowninshield*, 17 U.S. 122, 197 (1819). Defendant's purported oral modification is unenforceable. See *Radzanower v. Touche Ross & Co.*, 426 U.S. 148 (1976) (a specific statute controls over a general one).

## CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, Plaintiff respectfully requests that the Court grant summary judgment in its favor.
